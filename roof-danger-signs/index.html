Do I need a new roof? Key danger signs

What roof damage looks like, and how to tell whether you need a repair or a replacement.

Repair or Replace

If your roof is not very old and you spot a few loose or damaged shingles, you may be able to just replace them. But for broader or more extensive damage, it may be time for a new roof. The key is a regular roof inspection so you know what is going on up there.

Attic Leaks

Check your attic after severe weather. A leak could mean some shingles are damaged, the underlayment has failed, or the flashing that seals joints on your roof has deteriorated.

Dark Streaks

It may look like dirt, but dark stains on your shingles may be moss, lichen, or blue green algae. This may not require a new roof, but if and when you do replace your shingles, ask for StainGuard and StainGuard Plus protection.

Blistering or peeling paint

Poor ventilation may cause moisture to get trapped in your house, which may cause exterior paint to peel and blister.

Ceiling and wall stains

Leaks from outside the house or moisture trapped inside it can cause stains and possibly mold or mildew.

Sheathing decay

Poor ventilation and moisture in your layers of roofing materials can decay the sheathing, the layer of plywood that forms the foundation of your roof. If you notice a sagging ceiling, broken gutters, leaks, or missing patches of shingles, your sheathing may be damaged.

Shingle damage

When shingles reach the end of their useful life, you may notice they fall off or become cracked, brittle, or curled, which means it may be time for a new roof.

Tips on keeping your roof in good shape

  • Know how old your roof is. If you did not install it and you are not sure, check the inspection report from when you purchased your home, or ask a roofing company to estimate its age and condition.
  • Spot check your roof twice a year, once in spring and once in fall. Look for trouble spots, and also check your attic for damage. Not feeling up to it? Reaching out to Superior Roofing Specialists is always the best option.
  • Watch the homes around you. They experience the same weather yours does and were likely built around the same time. If your neighbors are getting new roofs, have yours inspected too.
  • After a significant weather event, like heavy winds or a hail storm, check for exterior damage as soon as it is safe.

How to do a roof inspection

Start inside

Look around your attic for signs of leaks, dark spots, holes, or sagging sheathing. Check your ceilings for stains, mold, or mildew.

Step outside

If you are comfortable climbing a ladder you can see your roof up close. If not, you can do a reasonably thorough inspection from the ground using binoculars. Clean leaves and debris out of gutters and downspouts, and check them for sagging or leaks. Look in hidden areas such as the underside of eaves and fascia for rot or signs of animal infestation.

Head to the street

From this distance, look for dark spots, missing shingles, or sagging. See if the exterior paint is blistering or peeling. If you live in an area with icy winters, keep an eye out for ice dams, the ridges of ice that form at the edge of your roof when water cannot drain off it.

Examine the roof

Check for cracked, torn, warped, or missing shingles and popped or rusted nails. Look for open seams or joints and make sure roof vents are not covered or clogged. Make sure pipes and roof penetrations, like your chimney or skylights, are sealed and have no exposed nails, and that flashing is not damaged or providing inadequate coverage.
